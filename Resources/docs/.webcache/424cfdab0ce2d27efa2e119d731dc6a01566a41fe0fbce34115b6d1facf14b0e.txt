# What's New

Big changes to the site — new sections, major expansions, and notable corrections — newest first. This is a *curated* list of the significant milestones, not a full commit log; the complete history lives in [the repository](https://github.com/laveeshb/linux-kernel-internals/commits/main).

## August 2026

- **New section: [USB](https://kernel-internals.org/usb/).** A full walkthrough of the USB subsystem — the host-scheduled bus model, [enumeration and descriptors](https://kernel-internals.org/usb/enumeration/), [URBs and the four transfer types](https://kernel-internals.org/usb/urbs/), [host controllers (xHCI) and gadget mode](https://kernel-internals.org/usb/xhci-gadget/), and [war stories](https://kernel-internals.org/usb/war-stories/) (BadUSB, a MIDI double-free, fuzzing the host stack).
- **Devices & Drivers, filled out.** The category launched with GPU/DRM, sound, input/HID, and TTY/serial queued up — all four landed this month: [GPU/DRM](https://kernel-internals.org/drm/) ([KMS and atomic modesetting](https://kernel-internals.org/drm/kms/), [GEM buffer objects and dma-buf](https://kernel-internals.org/drm/gem-dmabuf/), [command submission and the scheduler](https://kernel-internals.org/drm/command-submission/)), [sound/ALSA](https://kernel-internals.org/alsa/), [input/HID](https://kernel-internals.org/input/), and [TTY/serial](https://kernel-internals.org/tty/).
- **War stories, nearly everywhere.** Dedicated war-stories pages landed across most of the site this month — [net](https://kernel-internals.org/net/war-stories/), [security](https://kernel-internals.org/security/war-stories/), [locking](https://kernel-internals.org/locking/war-stories/), [VFS](https://kernel-internals.org/vfs/war-stories/), [BPF](https://kernel-internals.org/bpf/war-stories/), [drivers](https://kernel-internals.org/drivers/war-stories/), [virtualization](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/war-stories/), [DRM](https://kernel-internals.org/drm/war-stories/) (two rounds), plus first-class war-stories for the new USB/ALSA/input/TTY sections. Each incident is independently verified against a primary source — a real commit, CVE, or LWN article — rather than summarized from memory.
- **Citation integrity, twice over.** A site-wide sweep fixed broken and wrong LWN/docs.kernel.org links (including converting kernel commit citations from GitHub to git.kernel.org) and closed out the last 11 subsystems that had zero source citations at all. That work then surfaced a deeper problem: pre-existing illustrative code samples with fabricated struct fields, function signatures, and version claims. A page-by-page technical-accuracy audit of [virtualization/](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/) is underway to catch these — four pages fixed so far ([kvm-arch.md](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/kvm-arch/), [kvm-exits.md](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/kvm-exits/), [kvm-memory.md](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/kvm-memory/), [live-migration.md](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/live-migration/)), three more in progress.
- **New: [Hardware Evolution](https://kernel-internals.org/hardware-evolution/).** A companion to the [Linux Evolution](https://kernel-internals.org/linux-evolution/) timeline, tracing six moments where a hardware-landscape shift forced a new kernel abstraction — PCI's hotplug gap and the devfs-to-udev handoff, NAPI's multi-queue split, VT-x/SVM making KVM possible, WiFi's mac80211, the 2011 ARM Device-Tree crisis, and NVMe forcing the blk-mq rewrite — each backed by a primary source.
- **[Filesystems](https://kernel-internals.org/filesystems/), deepened.** Added [crash consistency and recovery](https://kernel-internals.org/filesystems/crash-consistency/), [iomap](https://kernel-internals.org/filesystems/iomap/), [FUSE](https://kernel-internals.org/filesystems/fuse/), and [war stories](https://kernel-internals.org/filesystems/war-stories/), plus a rewritten overview and primary-source citations across the existing pages.
- **[Block layer](https://kernel-internals.org/block/) & [tracing](https://kernel-internals.org/tracing/), expanded.** The block layer gained a life-of-a-block-I/O walkthrough, cgroup I/O control, observability, and war stories; tracing gained the ring buffer, trace events, BPF-for-tracing, and war stories.
- **Navigation & readability overhaul.** The 30+ subsystems are now grouped into themed category tabs with landing pages, a navigable sidebar, and larger, more readable text.

## July 2026

- **Build integrity enforced in CI.** Pull requests are now gated on strict builds with internal-link and anchor validation, so a broken cross-reference can no longer merge.
- **More memory-management depth.** Added the [physical memory model](https://kernel-internals.org/mm/memory-model/), the [kernel half of the address space](https://kernel-internals.org/mm/kernel-address-space/), and [GUP (Getting User Pages)](https://kernel-internals.org/mm/gup/).
- **Sourcing pass.** Sourced further-reading sections across the [architecture](https://kernel-internals.org/arch/arm64/) docs, and a refresh of the section landing pages.

## April 2026 — Depth pass

- **Stub sections grown to comprehensive coverage.** Deep-dive expansions landed across [arch/x86](https://kernel-internals.org/arch/x86/) and the [core kernel](https://kernel-internals.org/kernel/); [I/O and io_uring](https://kernel-internals.org/io/); [IOMMU](https://kernel-internals.org/iommu/) and [crypto](https://kernel-internals.org/crypto/); [livepatch](https://kernel-internals.org/livepatch/), [syscalls](https://kernel-internals.org/syscalls/), [modules](https://kernel-internals.org/modules/), and [time](https://kernel-internals.org/time/); [virtualization](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/), [debugging](https://kernel-internals.org/debugging/), [cgroups](https://kernel-internals.org/cgroups/), [IPC](https://kernel-internals.org/ipc/), and [power](https://kernel-internals.org/power/); and [arch/arm64](https://kernel-internals.org/arch/arm64/).

## March 2026 — From memory to the whole kernel

- **The site expanded from a memory-management reference into a whole-kernel one.** Two dozen new subsystem sections landed at once: the [scheduler](https://kernel-internals.org/sched/), [networking](https://kernel-internals.org/net/), [VFS](https://kernel-internals.org/vfs/) / [filesystems](https://kernel-internals.org/filesystems/) / [storage](https://kernel-internals.org/block/) / [IPC](https://kernel-internals.org/ipc/) / [syscalls](https://kernel-internals.org/syscalls/), [locking](https://kernel-internals.org/locking/) / [interrupts](https://kernel-internals.org/interrupts/) / [BPF](https://kernel-internals.org/bpf/) / [tracing](https://kernel-internals.org/tracing/), and [architecture](https://kernel-internals.org/arch/arm64/) / [security](https://kernel-internals.org/security/) / [drivers](https://kernel-internals.org/drivers/) / [virtualization](https://kernel-internals.org/virtualization/).
- **Memory management deepened.** NUMA-advanced topics, reclaim internals, modern mm (folio, maple tree, TLB, RCU), the first mm war stories, and boot/DMA coverage.
- **First site-wide citation audit.** Primary-source citations added across many subsystems, alongside an accuracy review spanning 85 documents.

## January 2026 — Launch

- **The site went live** as a deep [memory-management](https://kernel-internals.org/mm/) reference, paired with a history of [Linux's evolution](https://kernel-internals.org/linux-evolution/).
- Custom domain, automated deploys, and [contribution guidelines](https://kernel-internals.org/contributing/) established.
