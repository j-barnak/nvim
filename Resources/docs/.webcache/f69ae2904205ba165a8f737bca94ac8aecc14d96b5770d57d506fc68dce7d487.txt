### [Recap](#recap)

True, bf is unlike any programming language we’ll ever need or use. But it’s very simple, and thus a great way to try out some of the classic power tools in building languages.

-

  We saw how a [grammar](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#grammar) is the organizing principle of a language—a tool for explaining its structure. By following the grammar, we can light a path through the implementation. Our time spent writing out the grammar paid off by making the rest of the language easier to think about & snap together.

-

  Every Racket language has a [reader](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#reader); every reader has a read-syntax function; and every read-syntax function will contain some kind of [parser](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#parser). A parser converts a source string into a structured representation called a [parse tree](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#parse-tree).

-

  Rather than building a parser by hand, we used the brag language to convert a grammar into a parse function.

-

  We learned how a [tokenizer](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#tokenizer) cooperates with the parser to filter out irrelevant characters from the source string, and categorize others.

-

  In our expander, [macros](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#macro) were the glue that let us convert the parse tree produced by our reader into Racket functions that implemented the logic of the bf language.

-

  We learned how to turn a folder of Racket modules into a [package](https://beautifulracket.com/appendix/glossary.html#package), which allowed us to use the neater #lang bf notation at the top of a source file.

Parts of this tutorial were adapted from *[Fudging Up a Racket](https://github.com/dyoo/brainfudge)*, by Danny Yoo. Danny also created [ragg](http://docs.racket-lang.org/ragg/), which is the basis of [brag](http://docs.racket-lang.org/brag/). The “follow the grammar” principle is from Daniel P. Friedman and Mitchell Wand, [*Essentials of Programming Languages*](http://www.amazon.com/Essentials-Programming-Languages-MIT-Press/dp/0262062798).
