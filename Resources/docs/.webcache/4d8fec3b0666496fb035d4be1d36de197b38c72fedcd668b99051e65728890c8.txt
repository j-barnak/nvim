# angr: The Next Generation of Binary Analysis

*A tutorial submission by Ruoyu (Fish) Wang and Yan Shoshitaishvili, presented at the 2017 IEEE Secure Development (SecDev) Conference.*

## Introduction

The paper opens from a simple premise: "Software is becoming increasingly more complex, and vulnerabilities more subtle." As a result, "better approaches are required to effectively analyze modern binaries, efficiently identify deeply buried defects, and intelligently assist human analysts with specific software reversing tasks." The authors note that although many strong binary analysis techniques have recently emerged from both academia and industry, applying them has stayed difficult "due to the lack of a flexible and approachable binary analysis platform."

angr is offered as the answer. It is described as "the next-generation binary analysis platform developed by the SecLab of University of California, Santa Barbara." The authors characterize it as "flexible, easy to work with, cross-platform and cross-architecture," with "many techniques from academia already implemented and embedded inside."

The proposed course starts "about the fundamental underpinnings of angr, dynamic symbolic execution, and static binary analysis," then demonstrates "best practices in doing symbolic execution and data dependence tracking in angr," and finally shows "how angr can assist in bug hunting." All demonstrations are to be "performed on CTF challenges and real-world programs."

## Audience

The tutorial lists three prerequisites for participants: comfort "to work with Python, both through Python scripts and the IPython interactive shell"; "a working knowledge on x86/x64 assembly"; and "basic knowledge of binary analysis and reversing, both static analysis techniques and dynamic approaches." The stated outcome is that afterward the audience "should know how to install and use angr, and should have a direction on how to build tools on top of the framework."

## Reference Information

The paper points to three sources of documentation for angr: the angr book (docs.angr.io), the angr API reference (api.angr.io), and a collection of angr examples hosted in the angr-doc repository on GitHub.

## Tutorial Format

The authors propose a four-part layout:

1. **Binary analysis 101** — the goals of binary analysis and an overview of binary analysis techniques.
2. **Introduction to angr** — using angr from a Python interactive interface, loading a binary, "Static analysis: CFG recovery," and a "Symbolic execution demo/exercise."
3. **Performing basic binary analysis tasks** — "Vulnerability discovery with symbolic execution," "angr-assisted (or human-assisted) Binary exploitation," and "Automatic ROP chain generation."
4. **Digging deeper** — what symbolic execution can and cannot solve, "Performing symbolic execution efficiently," angr internals (its claripy component), and extending angr.

The authors add a practical caveat: they "hope to have two or three small hands-on demos, but it is hard to fit so many into 90 minutes."

## Tutors

The two presenters are described as "two of the leaders of the angr project." At the time of writing, Yan Shoshitaishvili is noted as an assistant professor at Arizona State University and Fish (Ruoyu) Wang as a PhD student in the security lab of UC Santa Barbara. The paper highlights their record: in 2013 they created MovieStealer, "a tool to automatically break the DRM of streaming media services"; in 2015 they analyzed backdoors in embedded devices; and in 2016 they proposed a new vulnerability discovery technique and "carried out a comparative survey of the existing state of the art in the field" (the "(State of) The Art of War" study). The authors are also members of Shellphish, described as "one of the top CTF teams," and they note that angr "was of great help" on many binary challenges they solved.
