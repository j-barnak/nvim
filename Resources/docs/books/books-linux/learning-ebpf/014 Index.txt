Colophon
The animal on the cover of Learning eBPF is an early bumblebee (Bombus pratorum),
a bumblebee species found in most of Europe (especially the UK) and parts of Asia.
Bombus pratorum builds nests above ground in fields, parks, and sparse forest, even
repurposing abandoned bird or rodent nests. The early bumblebee indeed emerges
early in the year, typically from March to July, but in southern England, worker bees
appear as early as February, making it quite common for two colony cycles to occur
in a single year.
This species of bumblebee is quite smaller than others. While there are slight varia‐
tions among queen, worker, and male drone types, the early bumblebee’s appearance
is generally black with a yellow collar, another yellow band on the abdominal seg‐
ment, and a reddish or dull orange tail.
Early bumblebees form colonies with queen and worker bees but, unusually, queens
use aggressive behavior rather than pheromones to establish dominance, using her
mandibles to head-butt the strongest worker bees to maintain control of the colony.
Worker bees forages for nectar and pollen of flowering plants such as white clover,
thistle, sage, lavender; drones are created late in the hive cycle and leave the nest in
search of new queens.
Many of the animals on O’Reilly covers are endangered; all of them are important to
the world. The cover illustration is by Karen Montgomery, based on an antique line
engraving from The Animal Kingdom Illustrated. The cover fonts are Gilroy Semibold
and Guardian Sans. The text font is Adobe Minion Pro; the heading font is Adobe
Myriad Condensed; and the code font is Dalton Maag’s Ubuntu Mono.
