Colophon
The animal on the cover of Making Embedded Systems is a great-eared nightjar. Great-
eared nightjars are members of the family Caprimulgidae and inhabit subtropical or
tropical moist lowland forests in Southeast Asia.
Great-eared nightjars are crepuscular, meaning mostly active at dusk and at night,
during which time they satisfy their diets of flying insects and moths. Their calls are a
distinctive, sharp “tissk,” followed by a two-syllable “ba-haw.”
These birds have pronounced ear tufts, which, in addition to their average length of
16 inches, make them more conspicuous among nightjars. Their soft gray and brown
plumage resembles their preferred habitat, leaf litter and bracken. They lay their eggs
either directly on bare ground or in leaf-litter nests on the ground. Nightjar nestlings
have been observed to be completely silent and motionless, which, in addition to
their leaf-colored plumage, may help protect them from danger while nesting.
